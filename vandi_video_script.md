# Vandi 3-Minute Video Script

Suggested total length: about 3 minutes

Suggested number of scenes: 7

This version puts more focus on the project itself:
- about 30 seconds on why vanadium matters
- about 30 seconds introducing vanadium and VRFB
- project section starts with the power system, then software features and user interaction

## Scene 1 (0:00-0:15)

**Narration**  
Climate change is one of the biggest challenges of this century, and renewable energy is part of the solution. But generating clean power is not enough. The real challenge is storing it safely and reliably, so we can use it whenever we need it most.

**Visual Description**  
Show bright sunlight over the Hong Kong skyline, rooftop solar panels, then a fast transition to sunset and night. Add a simple text question: "Clean energy is easy to generate. But how do we keep it?"

## Scene 2 (0:15-0:45)

**Narration**  
That is why vanadium matters. For renewable energy to work on a large scale, we need batteries that last longer, stay safer, and can be used again and again. Traditional lithium-ion batteries are powerful, but for large-scale storage they face problems like aging, recycling difficulty, and limited long-duration performance. Vanadium offers another path.

**Visual Description**  
Show solar energy being stored, then compare a conventional battery with a larger flow-battery-style system. Put short labels on screen: "long life", "safer", "reusable electrolyte", "better for large-scale storage".

## Scene 3 (0:45-1:15)

**Narration**  
Vanadium Redox Flow Batteries, or VRFBs, store energy in liquid electrolytes instead of solid battery materials. Two vanadium solutions are stored in separate tanks and pumped through a cell stack. Because the system uses vanadium in different oxidation states, it can charge and discharge repeatedly with strong stability, making it especially promising for renewable energy storage.

**Visual Description**  
Show a clean animation of two tanks, pumps, tubes, and the cell stack. Highlight that the energy is stored in the liquids, not inside a solid block battery. Introduce the name "VRFB" clearly on screen.

## Scene 4 (1:15-1:40)

**Narration**  
The chemistry behind this can be confusing, but basically, yellow solution on one side and violet solution in the other tank, wire them up and we get energy. It is just like magic, except the magic is electrochemistry.

**Visual Description**  
Show the actual experiment setup or a clear diagram of it: two tanks, pumps, tubing, electrodes, and external wires connected to a load. Use simple color coding for the two electrolytes. Put the equations on screen:

`Negative: V3+ + e- <-> V2+`

`Positive: VO^2+ + H2O <-> VO2^+ + 2H+ + e-`

Also show a simple overall message such as "Ion change -> electron flow -> usable energy".

## Scene 5 (1:40-2:10)

**Narration**  
To bring this technology closer to people, we created Vandi. The first part of Vandi is its power system. Instead of treating the battery as a hidden component, we made it the heart of the project. Vandi is powered by a vanadium-based energy storage system, turning a toy car into a hands-on demonstration of how flow-battery technology can power real devices.

**Visual Description**  
Reveal the Vandi car. Focus first on the hardware and power architecture: battery setup, wiring, power delivery, and how the energy from the vanadium system supports the car. Use close-up shots and labeled overlays such as "power source", "energy flow", and "system integration".

## Scene 6 (2:10-2:45)

**Narration**  
On top of that power system, we added intelligent software features to make Vandi interactive and engaging. Vandi can navigate automatically, recognize objects using a vision-language model, and detect obstacles with LiDAR. This allows the car not only to move, but also to sense, decide, and respond to its environment in real time.

**Visual Description**  
Show Vandi driving through a test track. Overlay route planning for auto-navigation, recognition boxes for VLM object recognition, and scanning lines or point-cloud style graphics for LiDAR obstacle detection.

## Scene 7 (2:45-3:00)

**Narration**  
Vandi is our way of making vanadium redox technology easier to see, easier to understand, and easier to remember. By combining sustainable energy storage with AI-powered interaction, we hope to show that vanadium can play an important role in the future of renewable energy.

**Visual Description**  
End with Vandi moving smoothly toward the camera while chemistry icons and AI overlays merge into one final frame. Finish with the project name and a closing message such as "Vandi: where intelligent systems meet sustainable energy."
